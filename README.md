I turned a girlhood dream into a website with AI, GPT-6 and vibe coding.

When I started OXNYMIA, I wanted to make a place for the details I have always loved: beautiful cards, flowers, silk and imagined worlds. It became 24 illustrated card faces in 12 pairs, each with its own front and inner page.

I brought the visual ideas and stories, then worked with AI to refine the pages, animation, interactions and sound through repeated experiments.

The tour begins with Moon Palace Jewels: peonies, a crescent moon, a moon rabbit and a mythic maiden. From there, you will find palace lanterns, koi, butterflies, silk fans and delicate gongbi-style brushwork. Lotuses, magnolias, irises, night-blooming flowers, cloud motifs, moon windows, jade pendants, peacocks and white deer appear throughout the collection.

I cared just as much about the materials: gold-edged flowers and enamel-like petals; the soft greens of jade, the luster of pearls, the iridescence of mother-of-pearl, and the light passing through crystal and colored liuli glass. Look closer for silk, shimmering brocade, fine embroidery inspired by Suzhou needlework and the delicate surface of a silk fan. I wanted these qualities to live in digital images.

Some flowers take on the colors of sugar painting, sugar sculpture, jam and sweets. Others belong to a Shanghai-inspired world of qipao, lace, magnolias and crystal spheres. Chinese pavilions sit beside fairy-tale castles, while celestial wheels, cats and moonlight add their own small stories. Flowers, swords and cranes bring a little sharpness to the softer scenes.

The details respond to you. Touch flowers or instruments to hear the guzheng, pipa, xiao and dizi flutes, guqin and bronze chime bells. Watch a maiden pick a peach, ribbons drift, lantern panels turn and costumes change. The final card opens into an invitation with a garden and a letter.

The inner pages also introduce what I hope to make next: women's stories and film, worlds and characters, AI models and applications, games, and projects that share Chinese culture with a wider audience.

I write stories and code. This website is a small beginning, and a home for things I have never wanted to forget.

Take your time and turn a card over. Which one would you open first?
